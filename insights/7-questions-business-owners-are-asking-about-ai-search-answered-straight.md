Your customers are already using ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews to find businesses like yours. Most owners know that. What they don't know is whether it changes anything they should actually do, or whether the whole thing is just noise.

This article answers the seven questions we hear most often. No jargon, no hype, just direct answers so you can decide what, if anything, to do next.

> **Quick orientation:** AI search and Google search are two different ecosystems. A business can rank on page one of Google and still be completely absent when someone asks an AI assistant for a recommendation. That gap is what this article is about.

## Question 1: Has SEO Become Irrelevant?

**Short answer:** No. But its job description has changed, and ignoring that change is costing businesses traffic they already earned.

SEO is not dead. Google still processes an estimated [8.5 billion searches per day](https://www.google.com), and organic rankings still drive real traffic for millions of businesses. The argument for SEO being dead is wrong.

What is true: SEO's share of the attention is shrinking. Since Google introduced AI Overviews, [organic click-through rates have dropped by as much as 61%](https://www.audienceintent.ai/insights/ai-search-visibility-2026) on queries where an AI summary now sits above the results. Fewer people click through when the answer is already on the page.

The more important shift is structural. Google and AI assistants are no longer the same thing.

-   **Google** ranks pages in a list. You optimize for that by building authority, earning links, and targeting keywords.
    
-   **AI assistants** (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok) generate answers. They pull from sources they trust, often without sending the user to your site at all.
    

These are separate ecosystems with separate rules. A business that treats them as the same thing will underperform in both.

**The practical takeaway:** Keep doing SEO. But recognize that it no longer covers the full picture. If your customers are asking AI assistants for recommendations in your category, and most of them are, then SEO alone is not enough.

## Question 2: What Makes an AI Platform Choose One Source Over Another?

**Short answer:** AI platforms cite sources they can crawl, trust, and verify. That comes down to three things: accessibility, authority, and relevance to the specific question being asked.

There is no public algorithm for how ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini decide what to cite. But the patterns are consistent enough to work with.

### The Three Factors That Drive AI Citations

**1\. Crawlability** If an AI crawler cannot access your content, your content does not exist to that platform. This means your site needs to be technically accessible, with no blocks in place that prevent AI indexing. More on this in Question 4.

**2\. Third-party corroboration** [Research shows that 85% of brand mentions in AI answers come from third-party sources](https://www.audienceintent.ai/insights/ai-search-visibility-2026), not from the brand's own website. AI platforms trust what others say about you more than what you say about yourself. A page that meanders through general information is less useful to an AI than a page that answers a specific question in the first paragraph.

## Question 4: Do AI Crawlers Require Their Own Setup to Access Your Site?

**Short answer:** Not always, but many businesses are accidentally blocking AI crawlers without knowing it. A quick technical check is worth doing.

AI platforms use their own crawlers to index content. ChatGPT uses GPTBot. Google's AI Overviews use Googlebot (the same one as regular search). Perplexity uses PerplexityBot. These crawlers follow the rules set in your site's `robots.txt` file.

### What Can Go Wrong

The most common issue: a `robots.txt` configuration that was set up years ago to block certain bots now inadvertently blocks AI crawlers too. This is not rare. Many site configurations use broad `Disallow` rules that sweep up newer crawlers that did not exist when the file was written.

A secondary issue is page speed and rendering. Some AI crawlers have trouble with JavaScript-heavy pages that require significant rendering time. If your most important content only appears after JavaScript loads, some crawlers may never see it.

**Three things to check:**

1.  Open your `robots.txt` file (yoursite.com/robots.txt) and look for any `Disallow` rules that would block GPTBot, PerplexityBot, or ClaudeBot.
2.  Make sure your key pages load quickly and that critical content is not hidden behind JavaScript rendering.
3.  Confirm your sitemap is up to date and submitted to Google Search Console, which feeds into several AI indexing pipelines.
    

None of this is complicated, but it does need to be checked. A business can do everything else right and still be invisible to AI simply because a crawler cannot get through the front door.
