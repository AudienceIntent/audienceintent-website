# How Old Can a Lead Be Before It Is No Longer Worth Contacting?

Lead age alone does not make a lead worthless. A lead from years ago may still be worth contacting if consent is documented, the contact information is usable, the original need is still relevant, and the business has a helpful reason to reconnect. Before writing off an old database, [estimate what your dormant leads could be worth](https://lostrevenue.audienceintent.ai).

## The Short Answer: Most Leads Are Not Too Old, They Are Just Poorly Segmented

Most businesses treat lead age as the primary filter. It is not. The more useful question is whether a given lead still meets the conditions required to make outreach appropriate and likely to be useful to the person receiving it.

The right filters are:

-   **Permission:** Is there documented consent to use the channel you plan to contact them on?
    
-   **Relevance:** Is the original service need still plausible, or has it clearly been resolved elsewhere?
    
-   **Intent signal:** Did they request a quote, book a call, or ask a specific question, or did they just fill out a generic form?
    
-   **Deliverability:** Is the phone number or email address still valid?
    
-   **Likely customer value:** Does the potential revenue justify the effort and the risk of a poor experience?
    

Old leads do carry one meaningful advantage over cold audiences: they already knew your business existed. They raised their hand at some point. That prior awareness lowers the barrier to a conversation. But it does not override consent requirements or substitute for good data hygiene.

**The practical implication:** a 30-month-old lead with documented SMS consent, a valid phone number, and a recurring service need is a stronger candidate for outreach than a 60-day-old lead with no consent record and an invalid email. Date of entry matters less than the conditions present at the time you plan to reach out.

## How Old Is Too Old? A Practical Lead-Age Framework

There is no universal expiration date for a lead. But there are useful planning ranges based on how lead age typically correlates with data quality, consent recency, and service relevance. Use this as a decision framework, not a compliance checklist.

| Lead Age | Default Recommendation | What to Verify | Suggested Approach | When to Stop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 90 days | Follow up now | Lead source, last contact, stated need | Direct, helpful follow-up | Opt-out, invalid data, explicit no |
| 3 to 12 months | Strong reactivation opportunity | Consent, service need, offer relevance | "Still looking for help with..." | No permission or irrelevant offer |
| 1 to 3 years | Reactivate selectively | Contactability, purchase cycle, prior engagement | Reintroduce value and ask permission to reconnect | Opt-out, stale data, sensitive context |
| 3 to 5 years | Triage before outreach | Consent records, number validity, category fit | Low-pressure check-in | Missing consent or invalid contact details |
| 5+ years | Case-by-case only | Explicit permission, data quality, legal/compliance fit | Re-permission or alternative channel | No documented basis to contact |

These are planning ranges. A five-year-old lead from a customer who booked a seasonal service every year and never opted out is a different situation than a five-year-old form fill with no follow-up history and an unverified phone number. The framework helps you triage; it does not replace judgment.

> **Key point:** The "when to stop" column is not optional. Every age band has hard stops. Consent issues and invalid data are not edge cases to work around — they are signals to remove the record from the campaign.

## The Five Signals That Matter More Than Lead Age

Once you accept that age is a secondary filter, you need primary filters. These five signals determine whether a dormant lead belongs in a reactivation campaign or in the archive.

### 1\. Consent and Communication Eligibility

This is the non-negotiable starting point. Before any automated marketing outreach, confirm that documented consent exists for the channel you plan to use. SMS, email, and phone calls each carry their own requirements, and those requirements vary based on the type of message being sent.

The practical steps:

-   Remove all opt-outs and do-not-contact records before building your send list
    
-   Verify that consent was captured at the source, not assumed from a general form fill
    
-   Confirm the consent covers the channel and message type you plan to use
    

The [FCC's consumer guidance on unwanted robocalls and texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts) provides a plain-language overview of what consumers can expect and what businesses are required to honor. Review it before launching any automated outreach campaign.

> **Disclaimer:** This is operational guidance, not legal advice. Consent requirements are fact-specific and vary by channel, campaign type, and jurisdiction. Consult qualified legal counsel before launching any reactivation campaign.

### 2\. Original Intent

Not all leads are created equal. A person who requested a quote, booked a consultation, missed a scheduled call, or abandoned a quote mid-way through is fundamentally different from someone who clicked an ad and filled out a generic contact form.

Segment your list by what the lead originally wanted, not just when they entered the CRM. A high-intent lead from 18 months ago is often a stronger candidate for reactivation than a low-intent lead from last week. The original action is the best proxy for genuine interest.

### 3\. Purchase Cycle and Service Relevance

Some service decisions are short-cycle and urgent. A burst pipe, a broken HVAC unit, or a pest infestation gets resolved quickly. If the lead is more than a few weeks old and the problem was urgent, it was almost certainly handled elsewhere.

Other decisions recur on longer cycles. Landscaping, HVAC maintenance, exterior painting, tax preparation, and similar services come up again every season or every few years. A lead from 18 months ago for a service with a 12-to-24-month natural repurchase cycle is not cold. It may be right on time.

**Ask before you outreach:** Does the underlying need for this service repeat? If yes, an old lead may be perfectly timed. If no, assess whether the window has passed.

### 4\. Data Quality and Contactability

A lead is only as good as the ability to reach the person. Before any campaign launches, verify:

-   Phone numbers (check for reassigned or disconnected numbers)
    
-   Email addresses (check for hard bounces and invalid domains)
    
-   Opt-out status across all channels
    
-   Duplicate records that could result in the same person being contacted multiple times
    
-   Ownership changes for business leads (the contact may no longer be at that company)
    

[List triage before outreach](https://audienceintent.ai/insights/the-biggest-missing-step-in-database-reactivation-list-triage-before-you-send-a-single-message) is the step most campaigns skip. Skipping it wastes budget, damages deliverability, and creates compliance exposure.

### 5\. Your Ability to Respond Quickly

This one is operational, not data-related, and it gets ignored constantly. Reactivation campaigns fail when someone replies with interest and no one follows up promptly.

Set a response process before the campaign goes live. Positive replies should route to a human within minutes, not hours. If your team cannot handle inbound responses quickly, the campaign will generate interest and lose it. The leads are not the problem. The follow-up is.

## When You Should Not Contact an Old Lead

This section is as important as the permission-to-contact criteria. Some records should not be in a reactivation campaign regardless of how old they are. Stop conditions are not technicalities — they protect the business and the people on the list.

Do not contact an old lead when any of the following apply:

-   **They opted out or asked not to be contacted.** This is a hard stop, not a judgment call. An opt-out on any channel must be honored immediately and reflected across your CRM.
    
-   **You cannot verify permission for the channel you plan to use.** If there is no documented basis for SMS, do not send SMS. If you are unsure, treat it as unapproved and use a re-permission approach instead.
    
-   **The contact information is invalid, reassigned, or clearly outdated.** Reaching someone who was reassigned a phone number is a real risk. Verify before you send.
    
-   **Their original inquiry is no longer relevant.** If they needed emergency roof repair two years ago, that need is resolved. Reaching out with a roofing offer is unlikely to be useful and may feel intrusive.
    
-   **The record involves a sensitive matter where re-engagement would be inappropriate.** Medical, legal, financial, and similar categories require extra care. When in doubt, exclude the record.
    
-   **Your team cannot respond promptly and accurately to replies.** If positive replies will sit unanswered for hours or days, the campaign will do more harm than good. Fix the response process first.
    

**The underlying principle:** outreach should be useful to the person receiving it. If you cannot make that case for a given record, remove it from the list.

## Why Old Leads Can Still Be More Valuable Than Cold Audiences

Cold lead acquisition starts from zero. The person has never heard of your business, never expressed a need, and has no prior relationship with you. You are interrupting them with an offer they did not ask for.

Old leads are different. They recognized a problem. They looked for a solution. They found your business and took an action. That prior intent is not erased by time.

> **Lead age is a filter, not a disqualifier. A 24-month-old lead with documented consent and a recurring service need can be more relevant than a brand-new cold prospect.**

The goal of reactivation is not to pressure someone into buying. It is to find out whether their situation has changed and whether your business can help now. That is a fundamentally different posture than cold acquisition, and it tends to produce a fundamentally different conversation.

### What This Means for Your CRM

Every record in your database represents a moment when someone raised their hand. Some of those moments are too old to act on. Many are not. The question is whether you have done the work to tell the difference.

Businesses that [understand the real ROI of lead reactivation](https://audienceintent.ai/insights/whats-the-real-roi-of-lead-reactivation-benchmarks-data-and-what-to-expect) consistently find that their existing database is a more cost-effective source of revenue than new lead acquisition — because the cost of the lead has already been paid, and the relationship, however dormant, already exists.

That does not mean every old lead is worth pursuing. It means the default should be evaluation, not dismissal. A CRM full of dormant records is not a dead asset. It is an unsorted one.

## How to Reactivate an Old Lead Without Sounding Desperate

The tone of a reactivation message is what separates a useful check-in from an awkward sales push. The goal is a genuine conversation, not a conversion event.

### Step 1: Triage the List First

Before writing a single message, clean the data. This step is not optional.

-   Remove all opt-outs and do-not-contact records
    
-   Flag invalid or unverified phone numbers and email addresses
    
-   Group remaining records by: original service requested, location, lifecycle stage (estimate request, consultation booked, no-show, etc.), and last meaningful interaction
    
-   Remove duplicate records
    

This work determines who belongs in the campaign and which message is appropriate for each segment. Sending the same message to everyone on a mixed list is one of the most common reasons reactivation campaigns underperform. For a detailed walkthrough of this process, see [the list triage guide](https://audienceintent.ai/insights/the-biggest-missing-step-in-database-reactivation-list-triage-before-you-send-a-single-message).

### Step 2: Lead with Relevance, Not a Pitch

The first message should reference what the person originally wanted. It should ask a question, not make an offer. Keep it short.

A simple example:

> *"Hi \[First Name\], you reached out to us about \[service\] a while back. Are you still looking for help, or has that been handled?"*

That is it. No discount, no urgency, no pitch. Just a relevant question that gives the person an easy way to say yes, no, or not yet. The relevance of the reference to their original inquiry is what makes this feel personal rather than automated.

### Step 3: Make the Next Step Easy

If someone replies with interest, the friction should be near zero.

-   Offer a simple reply option ("Just reply here and we'll get back to you")
    
-   Do not force a booking link into the first message
    
-   Route positive replies to a human who can respond within minutes
    
-   Keep the conversation going before introducing a calendar link or next step
    

The goal of the first message is a reply. The goal of the reply is a conversation. The goal of the conversation is a qualified appointment or sale. Collapsing all three into a single automated message is where most reactivation attempts fail.

## Common Mistakes That Make Dormant-Lead Campaigns Fail

Most dormant-lead campaigns that do not produce results fail for the same predictable reasons. These are not strategic failures. They are execution failures that happen before a single message is sent.

-   **Treating every old lead identically.** A missed call from three weeks ago and a form fill from three years ago are not the same situation. Segment before you send.
    
-   **Messaging without validating consent and opt-out status.** Sending to records with unverified consent is not just a compliance risk. It also produces worse results because you are reaching people who did not expect to hear from you.
    
-   **Sending generic "just checking in" copy.** This signals that you do not know who you are talking to. Reference the original service or inquiry. Specificity is what makes a message feel personal.
    
-   **Launching before cleaning the CRM.** Bad data produces bad results. Invalid numbers, duplicate records, and unresolved opt-outs should be removed before the campaign is built, not after.
    
-   **Failing to tailor the offer to the original service need.** A lead who asked about landscaping does not want a message about window cleaning. Match the message to the record.
    
-   **Waiting too long to respond to positive replies.** Speed of response is one of the most consistent predictors of whether a warm reply converts. Hours-long delays lose the window.
    
-   **Measuring sends rather than conversations, appointments, and revenue.** A high send volume with no downstream activity is not a result. Track what actually moves the business forward.
    

**The pattern behind all of these:** businesses launch reactivation campaigns the same way they launch broadcast marketing. Reactivation is not broadcast. It is a targeted conversation with someone who already knows you. The execution has to match that reality.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a five-year-old lead worth contacting?

Possibly, but only after confirming permission, data quality, relevance, and that you have a genuine reason to reconnect. A five-year-old lead who booked a recurring service and never opted out is a different situation than a five-year-old form fill with no engagement history. Evaluate the record on its own merits before deciding.

### Is it legal to contact leads from years ago?

Lead age is not the primary legal consideration. Businesses must evaluate consent, opt-out status, the channel being used, and applicable rules for that channel and message type. The [FCC's guidance on unwanted robocalls and texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts) is a useful starting point. This article is operational guidance, not legal advice. Consult qualified legal counsel before launching any automated outreach campaign.

### How often should you follow up with a dormant lead?

Use a short, respectful sequence with clear opt-out handling. Two to three touches over a defined window is a reasonable starting point. Stop if there is no engagement after the sequence. Repeated outreach to someone who has not responded is not persistence — it is noise, and it creates compliance risk.

### Should old leads receive SMS, email, or phone calls?

Choose the channel based on documented permission, the original relationship, data quality, and the type of message being sent. SMS typically produces higher open and response rates, but it requires explicit consent. Email has a lower barrier in some contexts but lower engagement. Phone calls are appropriate for high-value leads where a direct conversation is warranted and consent is in place. The channel should follow the consent, not the other way around.

### What should you say to a lead that went cold?

Reference the original reason they contacted you, ask whether the need is still relevant, and offer a low-pressure next step. Keep it short. The goal is a reply, not a sale. A message like "You reached out about \[service\] a while back — are you still looking for help, or has that been handled?" gives the person an easy way to respond without feeling sold to.

### When should a business stop contacting a lead?

Stop when the person opts out, the contact information is invalid, permission cannot be supported for the channel being used, the original need is no longer relevant, or repeated outreach produces no engagement. Any one of these conditions is sufficient reason to remove the record from active campaigns.

## The Bottom Line: Do Not Write Off Your Database Because It Is Old

Old leads should be evaluated, not automatically discarded. The date a record entered your CRM is one data point among many. It tells you almost nothing on its own.

The practical order of operations before any reactivation campaign:

1.  **Verify consent** for the channel you plan to use
2.  **Clean the data** — remove opt-outs, invalid contacts, and duplicates
3.  **Segment by intent** — group records by original service, engagement history, and purchase cycle
4.  **Launch a relevant campaign** — with messaging tailored to each segment, not a single blast to the full list
    

That process will shrink your sendable list. That is the point. A smaller, well-qualified list will outperform a large, unsorted one every time. The goal is not to contact everyone in your CRM. The goal is to contact the right people with the right message at the right time.

Not every old lead is usable. Not every old lead is profitable.
