Old leads are not dead leads. They are leads that did not buy yet.

The difference matters because the money is already in your CRM. You paid to generate those contacts. They raised their hand at some point. Something got in the way: timing, budget, a distraction, a competitor who followed up first. That does not mean the need went away.

**The first business to re-engage wins 78% of leads.** Most businesses never try.

The reason most re-engagement attempts fail is not the channel or the message. It is the approach. A generic blast to a cold list reads as spam, regardless of how it is delivered. What works is a reason to reach out that feels relevant to the lead, not convenient for you.

## Before You Send Anything: Segment First

The most common re-engagement mistake is treating a dormant list as one audience. It is not. A lead who requested a quote six weeks ago is a different situation from someone who filled out a contact form eighteen months ago and never responded to a single follow-up.

Segment before you touch anything. At minimum, split your list into three buckets:

-   **High-intent, recently cold (0-90 days):** These leads showed real buying signals. They asked for a proposal, booked a call, or requested a demo. Something interrupted the process. These are your highest-priority contacts and deserve a personal, specific message.
    
-   **Medium-intent, moderately cold (90 days to 1 year):** Leads who engaged but never converted. They opened emails, clicked links, or had a conversation that stalled. A value-first re-engagement sequence works well here.
    
-   **Low-intent, long-cold (1+ years):** Leads who came in through a form or ad and went quiet. Lower conversion probability, but still worth a structured campaign given the low cost of outreach compared to new lead acquisition.
    

> **Why this matters:** Reactivated leads close at 15-25%, compared to 5-10% for cold new leads. The ROI of re-engagement is strong across all three buckets, but the approach and channel should match the segment.

Once your list is segmented, pick your channel. Here are the main options.

## 7 Ways to Re-Engage Old Leads

### 1\. Email Re-Engagement Sequences

Email is the default re-engagement channel for most businesses, mainly because it is cheap and easy to automate. A well-structured sequence runs 3-4 messages spaced 4-7 days apart. The first message should be a soft check-in, not a pitch. The second adds value. The third makes a gentle ask. The fourth, often called a "breakup email," closes the loop and consistently generates the highest reply rates in the sequence.

**What email does well:** Low cost, easy to scale, works for long sales cycles where leads need time and information before deciding.

**Where it falls short:** Email reactivation rates run 1-3% for cold lists. Open rates for dormant leads land between 20-35%, meaning most of your list never sees the message. Deliverability issues compound over time as cold lists accumulate spam complaints and bounces.

### 2\. Phone Calls

A direct call works well for high-value, high-intent leads. If someone requested a proposal and went quiet, a call is appropriate. The challenge is reach: only about 28% of calls to cold numbers get answered. Of those, 62% convert to a meaningful conversation, which makes the math work for premium-ticket sales but not for high-volume lists.

Phone works best as a follow-up to another channel, not as the opening move.

### 3\. LinkedIn Outreach

For B2B leads, LinkedIn adds a personal, low-pressure touchpoint. A comment on a lead's recent post, a connection request with a brief note, or a direct message referencing a shared context can restart a conversation without feeling like a sales pitch. The key is that it must feel genuinely personal. Copy-pasted LinkedIn DMs perform about as well as cold emails.

**Best for:** B2B businesses with high-value leads where the relationship matters as much as the transaction.

### 4\. Paid Retargeting

Retargeting campaigns on platforms like Meta or Google serve ads to people who have previously visited your site or engaged with your content. It is a passive re-engagement method: you stay visible while the lead moves through their own decision process.

Retargeting does not initiate a conversation. It keeps you top of mind. It works best as a supporting layer alongside direct outreach, not as a standalone re-engagement strategy.

### 5\. Value-First Content Nurture

Sending genuinely useful content (a relevant guide, a case study, an answer to a question the lead actually had) can warm up cold contacts over time. This approach works for leads with long consideration cycles and businesses where trust is a prerequisite for purchase.

The downside: it is slow. Content nurture is a months-long strategy, not a way to recover revenue from a dormant list in the near term.

### 6\. Trigger-Based Outreach

Some re-engagement is most effective when tied to a specific event rather than a calendar. If a lead's company just announced an expansion, posted a new job listing in a relevant department, or showed up on your website again, that is a natural reason to reach out. The message references something real and specific, which makes it feel like service rather than interruption.

This approach requires intent data or manual monitoring and does not scale easily to large lists.

### 7\. Conversational SMS

This is where the data separates clearly from the other channels.

SMS delivers a 98% open rate. Most messages are read within three minutes of receipt. Response rates run 30-45%, compared to 6% for email. Conversion rates on SMS reactivation campaigns range from 21-40%, roughly double what email produces on the same list.

The reason SMS outperforms is not just delivery. It is format. A short, personal text message reads differently than a marketing email. It lands in the same place as messages from family and friends. When the message is written to feel like a genuine one-on-one conversation rather than a broadcast, the response rates reflect that.

## Why SMS Wins the Re-Engagement Channel Comparison

The performance gap between SMS and every other re-engagement channel is not marginal. Here is how the numbers stack up across the metrics that determine whether a campaign actually recovers revenue:

| Metric | SMS | Email | Phone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open rate | 98% | 20-35% | 28% answer rate |
| Response rate | 30-45% | 1.4-6% | 62% (if answered) |
| Reactivation rate | 16-20%+ | 1-3% | 13% |
| Conversion rate (reactivated leads) | 21-40% | 1-15% | Varies |
| Time to read | Under 3 minutes | Hours to days | Immediate or never |

The open rate comparison is the most telling. A 98% open rate versus 20-35% for email is not a modest improvement. It means SMS reaches nearly your entire list while email reaches roughly one in five. If your list has 500 dormant leads, SMS puts your message in front of 490 of them. Email reaches about 100-175.

### What Makes SMS Feel Personal Instead of Pushy

The channel alone does not guarantee results. The message has to be written like a text, not a marketing email. That means:

-   **Short.** Two to four sentences, not paragraphs.
    
-   **Personal.** Use the lead's name and reference something specific, even if it is just the service they originally inquired about.
    
-   **Conversational.** Ask a question. Give the lead something easy to respond to.
    
-   **No pressure.** The goal of the first message is a reply, not a sale.
    

A message that reads "Hey \[Name\], this is \[Business\]. You reached out about \[service\] a while back. Still looking for help with that?" performs because it sounds like a person, not a campaign.

### The Timing Factor

Leads contacted within 90 days of going cold are 10x more likely to reactivate than leads that have been dormant for two years or more. SMS works across both windows, but the best results come from acting before the lead's original problem has been fully solved by a competitor or simply forgotten.

If your list is older, that does not mean it is worthless. It means the message needs to acknowledge the gap in time and give the lead a reason to reconsider now. A new offer, a relevant result you delivered for a similar customer, or simply the acknowledgment that timing may have been off can restart a conversation that went cold.

## The Rules That Keep Re-Engagement From Feeling Annoying

Regardless of channel, the difference between re-engagement that works and re-engagement that damages your reputation comes down to a few principles.

### Have a real reason to reach out

Generic outreach ("just checking in!") performs poorly because it signals to the lead that you have nothing new to offer. The message needs a hook: a relevant result you delivered for a similar customer, a change in your pricing or offer, a seasonal reason the timing makes sense now, or an honest acknowledgment that you dropped the ball on follow-up.

### Limit your frequency

More touches do not equal more conversions. For cold leads, two to three outreach attempts over a 2-3 week window is a reasonable ceiling before moving them to a long-term nurture list or archiving them. Sending five messages in five days reads as desperation. It also trains your audience to ignore you.

### Make it easy to say no

The highest-performing re-engagement messages include an easy exit. "If the timing is off, just let me know and I'll leave you alone" is not weakness. It is honesty, and it generates replies from leads who appreciate not being pressured. Some of those replies turn into conversations.

### Respect compliance requirements

For SMS specifically: you need TCPA-compliant opt-in leads. This means the contacts on your list must have previously given consent to receive text messages from your business or from businesses in your category. Running SMS to a list without verifying opt-in status is both a legal risk and a deliverability problem. If your list was built through forms, ad campaigns, or direct inquiries where SMS consent was included, you are generally in a solid position. If you are unsure, review your original lead capture method before launching any SMS campaign.

### Do not re-send the same message

If the first outreach did not land, sending the same message with a different subject line will not fix it. Change the channel, change the angle, or change the offer. A lead who ignored your email sequence may respond to a text. A lead who ignored a pitch-focused message may respond to a question-based one.
