It’s an expensive cycle of running more ads, creating more content, and spending more on marketing, often just to keep the pipeline full. The pressure to find the next client can be all-consuming.

This endless pursuit is not only exhausting but often overlooks the most valuable asset a business already possesses: its past customers. These are the people who have already chosen you, paid you, and experienced your value firsthand. Instead of spending a fortune to convince a stranger to trust you, there's a more direct and profitable path to growth.

But what if your most valuable source of new revenue wasn't a new lead at all, but someone who already knows you?

## Your Best Prospects Aren't Strangers - They're Past Customers

The first step to smarter growth is recognizing a simple but profound truth: it is dramatically easier to sell to someone who has already bought from you. The data on this is clear and compelling.

According to research from Bain & Company, the probability of successfully selling to an existing or past customer is **60-70%**. In stark contrast, the success rate of converting a completely new prospect is only **5-20%**.

The reason for this massive gap is simple: trust and familiarity. A past customer already knows your brand, understands your value, and has overcome the initial hesitation that every new prospect feels. You don't need to introduce yourself from scratch; you just need to re-engage.

## The Financial Case is Overwhelming: A 7x Return on Investment

Beyond the higher success rate, the financial argument for focusing on past customers is undeniable. It isn't just a slightly better strategy; it's an exponentially more profitable one.

Database reactivation campaigns deliver an average **7x return on investment (ROI)** compared to new customer acquisition efforts. This means for every dollar you spend re-engaging your old database, you can expect seven dollars back - a return that is nearly impossible to achieve when chasing new leads. Furthermore, it is **5 times less expensive** to retain an existing customer than to acquire a new one.

For any business owner focused on the bottom line, the math is simple: reactivating your database leads to significantly higher profits with a much lower marketing spend. But the benefit isn't just a one-time cash injection. Reactivated customers also have a **3x higher lifetime value** than new acquisitions, turning a short-term campaign into a long-term, sustainable growth engine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How old can leads be before they stop being worth contacting?

The 3 to 24 month window is where most campaigns produce results. Leads under 18 months old respond best. Beyond 24 months, response rates drop sharply, though high-intent contacts (people who requested a quote, booked a call, or made a purchase) regularly reactivate even at 2 to 3 years. If you have older contacts, treat them as a low-volume test rather than a primary segment, and lead with a softer re-introduction since many won't remember you.

### Is it legal to text leads who haven't heard from us in years?

Age alone doesn't determine legality. Consent does. The TCPA sets no expiry on prior express written consent, so a two-year-old contact who opted into your SMS marketing when they inquired is generally still contactable. A recent contact from a third-party lead seller may not be, regardless of how fresh the record is. The requirement for Database Reactivation is straightforward: you need TCPA-compliant opt-in leads. If your list came from your own forms with a marketing disclosure, you're in the right territory. If you're unsure, consult a compliance attorney before sending.

### What reactivation rate should I realistically expect?

Results vary by list quality, industry, and how the contacts originally entered your database. Past customers who bought from you convert at the highest rate. Unconverted inquiries are next. Across our campaigns, we average a 20 to 30% reactivation rate. That number will differ for your business depending on how clean your list is, how long contacts have been dormant, and what your offer looks like. Anyone promising a specific rate before seeing your data is guessing.

### Will running a reactivation campaign damage my brand or burn my list?

It can, if done poorly. Sending one undifferentiated blast to thousands of contacts produces complaints, opt-outs, and carrier filtering. What prevents that: segmenting before sending, writing messages that read like a real person sent them, throttling volume, and responding to replies quickly. The campaigns that damage brands are the ones that prioritize volume over relevance. Done right, most contacts who aren't interested simply opt out, and the ones who are interested convert.

### How quickly do results come in?

Most replies arrive within 48 to 72 hours of the first send. Booked appointments typically concentrate in the first two weeks. The speed is one of the reasons SMS works for reactivation: it gets read immediately. We can have a campaign live within 3 to 5 days of onboarding, which means a business with a dormant list can realistically see its first booked appointments within a week of starting.
