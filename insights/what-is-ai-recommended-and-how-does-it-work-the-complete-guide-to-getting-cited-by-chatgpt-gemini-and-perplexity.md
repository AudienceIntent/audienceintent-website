## How Does AI Decide Which Businesses to Recommend?

AI systems can use information from your website and outside sources to answer recommendation questions. Clear business details, useful answers, and consistent information help them understand whether your business fits what someone needs.

For example, someone asking “Which food tour should I book in this city?” may care about location, tour length, dietary options, price, and availability. A page that answers those questions gives an AI system more useful information than a page that only says “unforgettable experiences.”

Useful information includes:

- **What you offer:** Specific services, products, prices where available, and who you serve.
- **Where you operate:** Accurate locations, service areas, hours, and contact details.
- **Answers to customer questions:** Clear explanations of choices, costs, limits, and next steps.
- **Supporting information:** Relevant business profiles, independent coverage, reviews, and other sources that may help confirm facts.

There is no published universal formula or fixed number of sources that guarantees a recommendation. Different systems can use different sources, and answers can change with the question, location, or timing.

For more detail, read [how AI decides which businesses to recommend](https://www.audienceintent.ai/insights/how-ai-search-engines-like-chatgpt-decide-which-businesses-to-recommend-and-how-to-be-one-of-them).

## What Does AI Recommended™ Include Every Month?

AI Recommended™ includes eight recurring parts, plus brand fixes. The work covers website access, technical improvements, useful content, daily tracking, and regular communication.

### 1. Open to Crawlers

AudienceIntent checks the settings and files that help crawlers access and discover your website, including robots.txt, llms.txt, and sitemaps. Useful content cannot help much if relevant systems cannot reach it.

The aim is to remove avoidable access problems and make important pages discoverable. These files do not force an AI system to index a page, cite it, or recommend your business. An llms.txt file is not a universal requirement for AI visibility.

### 2. Website Fixes: Schema, Sitemap, and On-Page Improvements

AudienceIntent makes website fixes that help explain your business and its pages more clearly. This includes schema, sitemap work, and on-page improvements.

Schema provides structured information about a business or page. On-page work can make headings, service descriptions, locations, and answers easier to understand. The goal is accurate, readable information—not adding markup that promises a ranking or citation.

### 3. Customer Question Research

AudienceIntent researches the questions customers ask when choosing a business like yours. Those questions guide content topics and updates.

A customer may ask which provider serves their area, what a service costs, how two options differ, or what to check before booking. Research helps focus the work on real buying questions rather than publishing articles simply to fill a schedule.

### 4. Four New Articles a Month

AudienceIntent creates four new articles each month to answer relevant customer questions. Clear explanations give both readers and AI systems useful material to work with.

Depending on the business, topics may cover service choices, costs, comparisons, common concerns, or location-specific needs. Each article should make its answer easy to find and support factual claims. Publishing four articles does not mean four guaranteed AI citations.

### 5. Four Older Pages Updated a Month

AudienceIntent updates four older pages each month. This keeps existing information useful instead of focusing only on new content.

Older pages may contain outdated details, incomplete answers, unclear service descriptions, or missed customer questions. Updating them helps the website present a more accurate picture of the business. The monthly commitment is four page updates, alongside the four new articles.

### 6. Weekly Visibility Report With 50 Prompts Tracked Daily

AudienceIntent tracks 50 customer-style prompts daily and provides a weekly visibility report. The paid service tracks ChatGPT, Google AI—including AI Overviews and AI Mode—and Perplexity.

Daily tracking gives a broader view than checking one question once. The weekly report helps you review changes over time without treating a single answer as a permanent result. AI answers can vary, so repeated observation matters.

### 7. Competitor Tracking for Up to Five Competitors

AudienceIntent tracks up to five competitors so you can compare your visibility with other businesses customers may be considering. This helps show whether an answer names your business, a competitor, or neither.

A competitor’s appearance is useful context, not proof that copying its content will produce the same result. The comparison helps identify questions and topics where your business needs clearer or more complete information.

### 8. Monthly Progress Call and Shared Slack Channel

AI Recommended™ includes a monthly progress call and a shared Slack channel. You have a way to discuss the work, ask questions, and review progress with the team.

The call connects the reporting with the work being done. Slack provides a shared place for communication between calls. You do not have to interpret a software dashboard and decide what to do on your own.

### Brand Fixes Are Included Too

AudienceIntent also optimizes your Google Business Profile and works on consistent business name, address, and phone information across major profiles. These details help people and AI systems identify the same business correctly.

This is specific business-information work. It is not digital PR, third-party content placement, review management, review-request automation, or an ongoing directory-management service.

## How Should You Read the Visibility Report?

Look for patterns across repeated checks, not a single favorable answer. A useful review asks whether your business appears more consistently for relevant questions and how that compares with competitors.

It also helps to keep three terms separate:

- **Mention:** An AI answer names your business.
- **Citation:** An AI answer references a source, often through a link.
- **AI traffic:** Someone visits your website from an AI platform.

These measures describe different things. More mentions do not automatically mean more visits, and more visits do not automatically mean more sales. A citation can reference your page without recommending your business.

## Is AI Recommended™ a Replacement for SEO?

No. AI Recommended™ focuses on visibility in AI answers, while SEO focuses on visibility in search results. The two share useful foundations, including accessible pages, clear content, and accurate business information.

A good Google ranking does not guarantee an AI recommendation. Equally, appearing in an AI answer does not prove that your website ranks well for the searches that matter to your business.

| Question | SEO | AI Recommended™ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main focus | Visibility in search results | Visibility in AI answers |
| Common measures | Rankings, search clicks, organic traffic | Mentions, citations, visibility across tracked prompts |
| Shared foundations | Useful content and accessible pages | Useful content and accessible pages |
| Guaranteed placement? | No | No |

Traditional search remains important. [Google reported more than 5 trillion searches a year in 2025](https://searchengineland.com/google-5-trillion-searches-per-year-452928), roughly 14 billion a day.

AI’s growth is a reason to review how customers find you, not a reason to abandon search. A [2024 Gartner forecast](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-02-19-gartner-predicts-search-engine-volume-will-fall-25-percent-by-2026-due-to-ai-chatbots-and-other-virtual-agents) predicted search engine volume would fall 25% by 2026 because of AI chatbots and other virtual agents. That was a forecast, not a measured result.
